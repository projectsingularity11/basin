Project Spotlight

Blue marine foundation is working to revive ocean life

The remote South Atlantic island of St Helena is home to a third of all the endemic species in British territories including 11 species of fish found nowhere else in the world. 

Blue Marine’s work

St Helena’s waters were designated an IUCN Category VI marine protected area in 2016, allowing only sustainable use. Blue Marine is working alongside the community to ensure that the area – all 444,916 square kilometres of it – is indeed protected and that the local islanders benefit from their exceptional natural resource. 

Fun fact

Endangered Whale sharks migrate to St Helena every year, and are even seen swimming amid the shallow moorings in Jamestown harbour. Arriving in equal numbers of males and females, anecdotal reports of whale sharks mating in the MPA – have resulted in St Helena’s waters receiving great interest from the global science community, as this has never been seen anywhere else in the world. Blue Marine is working hard to gather this mating evidence and to understand the breeding habits of these gentle giants to ensure they – and their aggregation grounds – receive the protection they require. 

Key Statistics

444,916 km2 Marine Protected Area

100% of island youth engaged in marine conservation

All local marine tour operators engaged in promoting sustainable tourism

Project Spotlight

Blue marine foundation is Rethinking the way we fish

An incredible underwater world

Located on the southeast coast of Scotland, Berwickshire’s waters are unique. Cold and warm currents converge, enabling a diversity of marine life rarely found elsewhere in the UK, from whales and dolphins to wolffish and kelp forests.

Unfortunately, the majority of the Marine Protected Area (MPA) in Berwickshire is open to bottom towed trawling and dredging with reports of unlawful fishing from trawlers being common.

The work of Blue Marine

Blue Marine is calling on the Scottish Government to protect its rich biodiversity for the benefit of all users.

To reshape marine management, we are focussed on: 

  • Strategic political engagement to secure sustainable fisheries and marine protection. 
  • Supporting low impact fishing and equitable use of marine resources 
  • Protecting Priority Marine Features (PMFs) located outside the current MPA network. 
  • Enhancing ecosystem recovery and biodiversity, including blue carbon habitats. 
  • Shifting perceptions and increasing support through education and outreach activities. 

Key Statistics

One of the largest populations of breeding grey seals in Northern Europe

Over 45,000 nesting seabirds

One of the top dive sites in the UK, with its own snorkel trail!

Project Spotlight

Blue marine foundation is Protecting the Maldives

The Maldives is 99% ocean

The future of the Maldives and its people are entirely reliant on the protection of their marine and coastal environment.

Yet, overfishing, tourism, pollution and coastal development are degrading the country’s reefs, seagrass meadows and mangroves. These habitats are losing their capacity to support the people who depend on them.

Blue Marine’s work

In collaboration with Maldives Resilient Reefs (MRR), we launched the #SeagrassIndustryLeaders Circle in 2025, following the success of our #ProtectMaldivesSeagrass campaign in 2019. This project calls on resorts to stop removing the seagrass around their islands to make clear lagoons for their guests.  

So far, 17 resorts have pledged to protect their meadows and champion the importance of seagrass as an asset for the tourism industry. MRR is working with the resorts to monitor progress and engage with the Tourism and Environment Ministry.  

Key Statistics

¼ of Maldives resorts have committed to protecting seagrass beds.

830,000 m² of protected seagrass beds.

200 students taken snorkelling for the first time.
